# Demo video script

One master video, 4 to 4.5 minutes, for YTH Live. A 2 to 3 minute cut of
it for DSH Hacks (marked below). English voice-over by Amin. Hands and
screen only: no faces, no testers, no real patient data. Only the made-up
sample record (Hana) is shown.

## Before recording

- Install the debug APK from the latest green CI run, or use the web demo
  in a phone-size browser window.
- Clear the app, so the first screen shows.
- Phone in do-not-disturb, full battery, clean background.
- Screen recorder on the phone, or a laptop screen recording of the web demo.
- For the camera shot: a second phone films the hand over the camera, a
  plain table, good light.
- Record the voice-over separately in a quiet room and lay it over the clips.

## Shot list and narration

Times are a guide. [DSH] marks shots kept in the short cut.

### 1. Team (0:00 to 0:30) [DSH: first sentence only]
Shot: title card "Cardiac Companion", then the app icon on the phone.

> Hi, I'm Amin Mohammed. TODO: age, school, city. I built Cardiac
> Companion. TODO: name anyone else on the team and what they did, or say
> "I work on it alone." I'm not on camera, so you'll see my hands and the app.

### 2. The challenge (0:30 to 1:00) [DSH]
Shot: slow scroll of the warning signs screen, sample record.

> In Ethiopia, many young people live with rheumatic heart disease. Many
> take warfarin, which needs regular INR blood tests, and a penicillin
> injection every few weeks. Missing either one can be dangerous. Most
> keep track on a paper card, and often a parent or a young caregiver
> keeps it for them.

### 3. What it is (1:00 to 1:20) [DSH]
Shot: first screen, tap the language list: English, Amharic, Afaan Oromo.

> Cardiac Companion is a medicine record for them. It works with no
> internet and no account, in English, Amharic and Afaan Oromo, with
> Ethiopian dates and the Ethiopian clock. Nothing leaves the phone.

### 4. Sample record and tour (1:20 to 1:45) [DSH]
Shot: tap "Try the sample record", let two or three tour stops play.

> To show it, I'll use a made-up patient, Hana. A short tour explains
> each part.

### 5. Today and warfarin (1:45 to 2:15) [DSH]
Shot: Today screen, warfarin dose and tablets, mark one dose taken. INR
list against the range.

> Today shows the warfarin dose exactly as the doctor wrote it. The app
> never works out or changes a dose. INR results are shown against the
> range the doctor set.

### 6. Penicillin and medicines (2:15 to 2:40)
Shot: penicillin countdown, the screen for the nurse, medicines list with
tablets left.

> This counts down to the next penicillin injection and keeps a record
> the nurse can see. Medicines are listed by time, and it warns before
> tablets run out.

### 7. Camera pulse (2:40 to 3:10) [DSH]
Shot: second phone films a fingertip covering the back camera and light,
then the screen with the "not a medical device" line in view.

> You can estimate your pulse with the phone camera and a fingertip. This
> is an estimate only. It is not a medical device, and we have not yet
> tested it against a pulse oximeter. If the signal is poor, it shows no
> number at all.

### 8. Visit summary and privacy (3:10 to 3:35)
Shot: make the visit summary PDF, then Settings: backup and PIN lock.

> Before a visit, it makes a one-page summary for the doctor. Records stay
> on the phone, with an encrypted backup and an optional PIN.
